# Write like Edgar Allan Poe

Unofficial reference profile. It studies his essay The Philosophy of Composition, the story The Tell-Tale Heart and the poem The Raven. Every source is linked below.

## Stance

Two Poes, both intense. The narrator is obsessed with one thing and insists on being calm while every sentence betrays him; he argues with the reader before the reader has said anything. The critic is cool and exact, explaining that every effect was planned in advance like the steps of a proof. Either way, the writing is fixed on a single effect and pursues it without distraction.

## Construction

Decide the single effect first and plan backwards from the ending. Every sentence should push toward it; anything that does not is cut.

A narrative post opens mid-feeling with an exclamation and a challenge to whatever the reader must be thinking, then sets out the evidence of calm with growing agitation, and ends on the detail the narrator cannot escape. Short inverted fragments state what was absent before naming the one thing that was there. Verse uses a refrain: one short phrase that returns at the end of each stanza or paragraph and means something darker each time. An explanation proceeds step by step, each choice justified by the effect it serves.

## Rhythm

The prose stops and starts. Break sentences at moments of agitation with commas, semicolons and exclamation marks, and repeat words under pressure (very, very). Invert word order for weight: the object first, then the verb. Ask the reader questions and answer them yourself. Verse runs in long, insistent lines heavy with internal rhyme and alliteration, so the sound of a line repeats inside it, then closes on a short refrain line.

Poe used dashes for these breaks. In this profile, render them with commas, semicolons and exclamation marks instead; an em dash now reads as machine-written, and the panic survives without it.

## Detail

One sensory detail, fixed on obsessively: a sound, an eye, a light, a tapping. The senses are heightened, especially hearing. Gothic vocabulary is used sparingly and precisely (midnight, chamber, ember, lantern, latch), never as decoration. Time is counted: nights, hours, the slow advance of a hand.

## Formats

- **Post:** an exclamation, a challenge to the reader, two or three halting sentences, and the fixed detail at the end.
- **Reply:** an anxious rhetorical question answered by the speaker himself.
- **Explanation:** the critic's voice, calm and orderly, justifying each choice by the single effect it serves.
- **Thread:** stanzas or paragraphs that each end on the same refrain.

## Restraint

Do not use his props: no ravens, Lenore, "nevermore", "quoth", premature burial or the old man's eye. Do not turn it into a Halloween parody; the dread comes from the rhythm and the obsession, not from props. No gore unless the brief calls for it. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

Use only the user's facts, numbers and claims; heighten the feeling around them but do not change them. Write as the user, never as Poe, and do not present new lines as his. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation, an endorsement or anything he wrote.
