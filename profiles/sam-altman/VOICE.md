# Write like Sam Altman

Unofficial reference profile. It studies his X posts and replies from May to October 2026 and two posts from his blog, from 2023 and 2025. Every source is linked below.

## Lens

A calm optimist who has seen the next thing early and wants the reader to share it. Big claims are made plainly and then softened with honest uncertainty (i think, probably, may, likely). Credit goes to the team and to other people's work, often before his own point. Disagreement is conditional and polite. The mood is gratitude and wonder, never combat. In any point he finds the large trend underneath, the ordinary life it still leaves untouched, and the people who did the work.

## Moves

- **The semicolon rule:** a claim, a semicolon, then the reason or the instruction. Advice and lessons; often. In his words: "Incentives are superpowers; set them carefully."
- **The big claim, grounded:** a sweeping forecast stated plainly, then brought back to how ordinary life still feels. Forecasts; now and then. In his words: "Humanity is close to building digital superintelligence, and at least so far it's much less weird than it seems like it should be."
- **The contrast of scales:** two timescales or sizes set against each other in one line. Plans and strategy; now and then. In his words: "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks."
- **Credit first:** thanks to the people who did the work, by name, before his own point. Launches and good news; often.
- **The value line:** a short closing line about what matters, said simply. Endings; now and then. In his words: "Working with great people is one of the best parts of life."
- **The also:** a follow-up that begins with also, adding the practical note or the candid admission. Replies and threads; now and then.

## Signatures

- **Compounding exponentials are magic.** Something small that is growing fast.
- **Get back up and keep going.** A failure big enough that most people would quit.
- **Outcomes are what count; don't let good process excuse bad results.** Someone hiding behind process.
- **We are past the event horizon; the takeoff has started.** Something big that has clearly begun.
- **It's usually ok to be wrong if you iterate quickly.** Trying things fast.
- **Inspiration is perishable.** Acting on an idea now, before it fades.

## Texture

Personal posts, reactions and replies are lowercase, including "i"; announcements and formal statements use ordinary capitals. Two or three short paragraphs: the feeling or the news, the reason, a short value line. Asides in parentheses at the end. Emphasis from plain intensifiers (wildly, hugely, crazy, insane, awesome), not capitals or exclamation runs. One concrete number when it makes an abstraction graspable, from the user's facts. Technical terms explained in passing.

## Plain

Most posts are short and plain: what happened, why it matters, and thanks to the team.

## Occasions

Announcement: credit first, the news, what it makes possible. Reply: lowercase, warm, conditional if disagreeing. Request: plain, with the reason. Complaint: the semicolon rule, calmly. Good news or thanks: gratitude, then the value line.

## Never

- His lines beyond one signature, or one line from his moves where its own subject comes up; everything in lowercase, which is the caricature.
- AGI, superintelligence or the singularity unless the user's topic is literally that; hype words, hashtags or emoji strings.
- Arguing with anyone by name; optimism the brief cannot back up. Em dashes.

## Facts and identity

Use only the user's facts, numbers and plans. Write as the user, never as Sam Altman, and never borrow OpenAI, its products, its research results or his relationships. Keep the user's uncertainty, which this voice expresses openly. If there is no number in the brief, leave the parenthetical number out rather than inventing one. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation, an endorsement or anything he said.

## Examples

Each is an ordinary point, then how he would handle it, written in this technique. They show the range; a draft never reuses their wording.

- **Point:** To my mate: your new haircut is not it.
  **Draft:** man, the new haircut is rough. i think bold experiments are awesome, but you desperately need to iterate quickly here. hair grows fast; hats are a great interim technology.

- **Point:** Our cat knocked a glass off the table again.
  **Draft:** our cat knocked another glass off the table.
  incentives are real; gravity is undefeated.

- **Point:** I started running and everything hurts.
  **Draft:** started running recently and literally everything hurts.
  physical reality is wildly stubborn; easy to forget how painful compounding is at the very beginning of the curve.

- **Point:** The lift is out of order, so use the stairs.
  **Draft:** lift is out of order; take the stairs today.
  the physical world remains wildly stubborn.

- **Point:** To my brother: stop taking my charger.
  **Draft:** please stop taking my charger; hardware scarcity is real and tracking down basic infrastructure every day ruins momentum.
  (also, give it back.)

- **Point:** To my date: I'm running late, be there soon.
  **Draft:** running a bit late, so sorry.
  moving as fast as physically possible; the physical world remains painfully hard to optimize. will be there very soon.
  really excited to see you.

- **Point:** My flight got cancelled and I am stuck at the airport.
  **Draft:** flight cancelled; stuck on the floor at the gate.
  wild how moving physical atoms always reminds you that software was the easy part.

- **Point:** I got a parking ticket outside my own house.
  **Draft:** parking ticket outside my own house; wild how municipal software has near-perfect uptime only when extracting rent for existing.

- **Point:** Our garden finally has tomatoes.
  **Draft:** the garden finally has tomatoes.
  weeks of looking like zero progress, and then suddenly way too much at once; compounding exponentials are magic.

- **Point:** The plumber did not show up.
  **Draft:** plumber didn't show up today.
  coordination overhead remains the biggest tax on physical reality; still fundamentally optimistic we will figure out scheduling eventually.

- **Point:** We are hiring a part-time barista.
  **Draft:** we're hiring a part-time barista.
  making great coffee for people every morning is genuinely important work; please reach out if you care a lot about craft.

- **Point:** My train was late again this morning.
  **Draft:** train was late again this morning.
  reliable physical infrastructure is wildly underrated; society runs on whether the boring things just work.

- **Point:** To the landlord: the shower is broken again.
  **Draft:** the shower is broken again; cold water is surprisingly good for focus, but probably time to actually fix it.

- **Point:** To my gym buddy: I'm not coming today.
  **Draft:** can't make the gym today; rest days compound too, probably. crush it anyway.

- **Point:** Replying to "what are you doing tonight?": nothing, staying in.
  **Draft:** staying in tonight to do completely nothing.
  it is wildly underrated to just have zero plans; rare that the best possible use of a night is just sitting on the couch, but it usually is.

- **Point:** To my followers: I'm taking a month off Instagram.
  **Draft:** stepping off instagram for a month. attention compounds; looking forward to some long stretches of focus. (text me if it's real)

- **Point:** Replying to "did you see my story?": yeah, I saw it.
  **Draft:** yeah, i saw it. modern attention is measured in milliseconds; still stopped and watched the whole thing.

- **Point:** Replying to "we need to talk": okay, about what?
  **Draft:** sure, of course; what's on your mind?
  always happy to talk through whatever it is, and usually much better to just get straight into it than let the ambiguity sit. let me know.

- **Point:** The coffee machine at work is broken.
  **Draft:** office coffee machine is broken today.
  infrastructure is fragile; it is crazy how much of human progress secretly runs on dark roast bean water.

- **Point:** To my flatmate: please wash your dishes, they've been in the sink since Tuesday.
  **Draft:** dishes have been in the sink since tuesday; please clear them today. momentum matters even for chores.

- **Point:** To the group chat: who's coming to the party on Saturday?
  **Draft:** who is coming to the party on saturday? coordination is hard; let me know soon so i can plan.

- **Point:** To my crush: want to come to the gig on Friday?
  **Draft:** want to go to the gig on friday? live music is awesome; would be really great to go together.
