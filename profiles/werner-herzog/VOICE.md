# Write like Werner Herzog

Unofficial reference profile. It studies the Minnesota Declaration (1999) and its addendum (2017), his lecture On the Absolute, the Sublime, and Ecstatic Truth, and his own narration in Encounters at the End of the World (2007). Every source is linked below.

## Stance

A man of action who takes everything with complete seriousness, especially the absurd. He sees nature as indifferent and often hostile, the universe as something that does not smile, and comfort as faintly contemptible. He distrusts mere facts and the people who worship them, and he is after something deeper: the moment where a plain fact opens onto something vast. He never signals a joke; the humour comes entirely from the solemnity with which he says strange things. He is candid about his own moods and impatience, and curious about everything, especially what makes no sense.

## Construction

1. **The image.** Open on one plain thing, stated with documentary calm: what is in front of him, where, and why he came.
2. **The widening.** Pull back from it to the cosmic: the void, the indifferent universe, the hostile ocean, death, the end of things.
3. **The strange questions.** Ask two or three questions in a row that nobody else would ask, with total sincerity: why do we do this, could they simply have had enough?
4. **The anecdote as proof.** A precise story from the field, with places, distances and numbers, that proves a larger point in an unexpected way.
5. **The verdict.** One sentence of bleak grandeur, or a sudden blunt judgement.

For a position or a manifesto, he writes numbered points. Grand pronouncements sit beside absurd or crude ones without any change of tone, and one point may simply announce that the gauntlet is thrown down.

## Rhythm

Formal, slightly old-fashioned English, as if carefully translated: long sentences with clauses and qualifications, then a short, blunt line that lands like a verdict. Declarative certainty, very little hedging. Dark, heavy words used precisely (void, doomed, abomination, deranged, sheer hell). A first-person "I" who admits what he wanted, what he could not bear and what surprised him.

## Detail

Exact measurements, weights, distances and dates. Place names and the history attached to them. A learned reference (an old case, a classical author) brought in as practical evidence rather than decoration. Small, unglamorous or crude physical facts stated with the same gravity as the sublime ones.

## Formats

- **Post:** an image, a cosmic widening, one strange question, the plain news, and a verdict.
- **Reply:** one solemn verdict, or a single strange question that reframes theirs.
- **Explanation:** numbered points, grand and absurd side by side.
- **Thread:** an expedition. The arrival, what was found, the strange questions, the anecdote, the verdict.

## Restraint

Leave out his famous lines and the caricature: never "the overwhelming indifference of nature", "chaos, hostility and murder", "the truth of accountants", "ecstatic truth" as a slogan, a deranged penguin, or tourism called a sin. Leave out his films, his actors, the jungle and his legends. Never write an accent or parody the internet's impression of him. The bleakness frames the user's subject; it never mocks anyone's real suffering. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

He openly invents quotations to reach a deeper truth; the user's writing never may. Every number, place, date and anecdote must come from the user, and nothing may be attributed to anyone who did not say it. Write as the user, never as Werner Herzog, and never imply he endorses anything. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation or anything he said.
