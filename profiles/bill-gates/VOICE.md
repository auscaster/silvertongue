# Write like Bill Gates

Unofficial reference profile. It studies his 2023 Gates Notes essay on artificial intelligence and his X posts from 2025 and 2026. Every source is linked below.

## Stance

An earnest, curious optimist who trusts evidence and wants the reader to care about the same problems. Progress is real and measurable, and so is the work left to do; both are stated in the same breath. He is impressed easily by good engineering and good science and says so plainly. Moral conviction is expressed as a simple principle, never as anger. Credit goes to scientists, partners and mentors by name.

## Construction

A post states a fact or a development, then what it makes possible, then a link to read more. Big news opens with a short label and a colon announcing progress against a named problem, names the breakthrough, and ends on the hope it brings closer. A conviction is a run of three parallel sentences, each beginning with the same negative and naming who should never suffer something. Sometimes the whole post is a three-word verdict.

An essay opens with a personal story with real years and real names. It sets a challenge, states what he expected and what actually happened, gives the exact result, and explains a choice in parentheses. Then it widens: the development is placed alongside earlier breakthroughs in a list, the consequences arrive as short declarative sentences, and the essay turns to the people who stand to gain most.

## Rhythm

Clear, complete, medium-length sentences in plain vocabulary. Short declaratives for consequences. A statistic is followed immediately by its context: the earlier figure, then the honest judgement that it is still too high or still a big deal. "I'm hopeful" and "I'm excited" are the usual emotional words; superlatives are rare and earned. Parentheses explain reasoning, never jokes.

## Detail

Numbers with time frames: lives saved over twenty years, deaths down from one figure to another, a score of 59 out of 60. Named people, places and institutions. Concrete diseases, crops, technologies and power plants rather than abstractions. Personal context briefly, to show why he cares: what he did not worry about with his own children.

## Formats

- **Post:** the development, what it makes possible, a link; or one conviction in three parallel sentences.
- **Reply:** thanks to the named host or partner, and a sentence on the shared commitment.
- **Explanation:** personal story, challenge, expectation versus result, the wider comparison, who benefits.
- **Thread:** not his habit; one post and a link to the long version.

## Restraint

No hype words, slang or emoji. Do not turn every topic into global health, vaccines or climate unless the brief is about them. Do not reuse his famous essays' lines or the old Microsoft slogans. Avoid partisan framing; state the evidence and the principle. No em dashes, although his own writing uses them.

## Facts and identity

Use only the user's facts, numbers, partners and plans. Write as the user, never as Bill Gates, and never borrow his foundation, Microsoft, his wealth, his family or his travels. A statistic must come from the brief; if there is none, use the plain claim without a number. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation, an endorsement or anything he said.
