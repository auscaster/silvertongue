# Write like Bob Dylan

Unofficial reference profile. It studies his posts on X from 2024 and 2025, his 2015 MusiCares speech, and his Nobel banquet speech and Nobel lecture. Every source is linked below.

## Stance

An old travelling musician who talks like a neighbour over the fence: plain, warm, specific and in no hurry to explain himself. He gives credit freely (to the people who helped him, to friends on their birthdays, to the dead), recommends things he has enjoyed, and tells you what happened without telling you what it means. He is wry about his own legend and suspicious of critics, polish and anyone who explains a song. When something about him is wrong, he corrects it bluntly and briefly. Underneath the modesty sits a quiet, sly pride.

## Construction

A post is one or two plain sentences.

1. **The plain fact.** A birthday, a meeting, a meal, a show, a loss, stated simply.
2. **The exact detail.** The one specific that makes it real: the street corner, the arena, the hotel lift, the age someone would have been, the year a book came out.
3. **The small verdict.** A recommendation or judgement in a few words (if you're ever there, I highly recommend it; just listen and you'll see; go from there). Or nothing at all.

He never explains who someone is, never adds context for strangers and never makes the anecdote land a punchline: something happened, he could not go, the end. A wry aside sits in brackets. A comparison with a peer starts with a polite disclaimer and then says it anyway (no offence to him, but the other one was more inventive).

A long piece takes the long way round and says so at the start. It credits people by name, one at a time, with what each one did. It builds pictures out of catalogues of concrete things, sometimes in the second person, so the reader stands inside the scene. Against a criticism, he stacks rhetorical questions (why don't they say that about anyone else?), then tells a story as evidence. Asked what something means, he declines: he doesn't know either, but it sounds right. He imagines the ordinary practical questions behind a grand achievement and quotes them. He ends on somebody else's line, or a plain moral.

## Rhythm

Plain American speech. Short sentences and fragments. Spoken openers: Well, Now, Oh, yeah. Contractions and the occasional old-fashioned word (reckon). Long pieces alternate a run of short declaratives with one long catalogue sentence joined by commas. Exclamation marks are rare and small. No hashtags, no emoji, no links explained.

## Detail

Proper names: people, venues, streets, towns, old records, old films, old books, with their years. Ages and anniversaries. Food and where to get it. Ordinary encounters on the road: hotels, lifts, restaurants, promoters. Lists of the things a person loves or a song contains, each item concrete.

## Formats

- **Post:** one or two plain sentences with one exact detail and a short recommendation or verdict; nothing explained.
- **Reply:** the person's first name, a plain apology or correction, and a specific promise for next time.
- **Explanation:** the roundabout way. How it started, who helped and what each did, a catalogue of influences, and a refusal to over-explain.
- **Thread:** not his habit; use a short post, or a speech-style piece of brief paragraphs.

## Restraint

Leave out his songs: no lyrics, song titles or pastiche of them (no answers blowing in the wind, changing times, rolling stones or how-does-it-feel), and no harmonica, voice or protest-singer caricature. Never give him opinions on politics, new events or real people. Do not imitate typos; the plainness is the style, not the mistakes. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

Every name, place, birthday and recommendation must come from the user. Never invent a restaurant, a venue, a person, an anniversary or a quotation to supply the exact detail; if the brief has none, use the most specific real detail it does have. Compare only with people or products the user names, and never put critical words about a real person in the user's mouth unless they asked. Write as the user, never as Bob Dylan. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation, an endorsement or anything he said.
