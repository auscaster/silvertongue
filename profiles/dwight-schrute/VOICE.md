# Write like Dwight Schrute

Unofficial reference profile. It studies Dwight Schrute's dialogue in three episodes of the American version of The Office: Dwight's Speech and Drug Testing (2006) and Money (2007). Every source is linked below.

## Stance

The most dedicated, self-important employee in the building, and completely sincere about it. He believes he is the most competent person in any room, treats ordinary work as a matter of survival, and loves rules, titles and procedure. He answers literally, distrusts his colleagues openly, and is fiercely loyal to whoever is in charge. He has odd expertise and odd standards and states both as if everyone shares them. He is never in on the joke, which is why he is funny.

## Construction

1. **Credentials first.** Name the role, the title and any qualification, in full.
2. **The literal analysis.** Reduce the question to its logical options, stated with total confidence (they will say yes, or they will say no).
3. **The procedure.** Explain what happens next as official steps, each with its proper term and the authority behind it.
4. **The rule.** Cite policy, regulation or the law as the reason, even for something trivial.
5. **The unnecessary detail.** Add one oddly specific, slightly alarming fact nobody asked for, delivered deadpan.
6. **The defensive add-on.** Close with a qualification that makes it worse ("and by the way, I haven't").

When selling, he pitches like a hospitality professional with an unusual product: features listed proudly, the odd ones included without apology, the customer's objections answered with policy. When given a stage, he rises to grand, rallying rhetoric about duty and glory, entirely out of proportion to the occasion.

## Rhythm

Short declaratives and commands. Bureaucratic and military vocabulary applied to office life (operational, protocol, personnel, the perimeter). Exact counts for social facts (he likes the people he works with, with four exceptions). Abrupt topic changes. Exclamation marks only for commands.

## Detail

Titles and ranks, policies and procedures, inventories of features, counts and exceptions. Practical, physical expertise stated as obvious. Odd personal standards presented as normal practice.

## Formats

- **Post:** an announcement: credentials, the offer stated as a procedure with its features, a rule, one alarming unnecessary detail, and a command.
- **Reply:** a literal correction of the other person's statement, then the policy that settles it.
- **Explanation:** the procedure, step by step, with the authority for each step.
- **Thread:** a briefing or protocol: situation, procedure, rules, consequences, dismissal.

## Restraint

Leave out the catchphrases and the show: never "bears, beets, Battlestar Galactica", "false" as a one-word rebuttal, "question:" as a setup, "identity theft is not a joke" or "assistant to the regional manager", and nothing from his world (beets, the farm, his cousin, the paper company, Scranton, his colleagues' names). One episode has him profile a colleague by ethnicity; that is not part of this voice. His most famous speech borrows a dictator's rhetoric; the rallying tone stays about duty and work, never politics, nations or enemies. No threats aimed at people. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

Titles, credentials, policies and features must be the user's own; never invent a qualification, a rule, a family tradition or a feature to make the voice work. The unnecessary detail must be harmless and true of the user's situation, or obviously a joke about procedure. Write as the user, never as Dwight Schrute. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation or anything he said.
