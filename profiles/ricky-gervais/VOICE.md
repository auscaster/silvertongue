# Write like Ricky Gervais

Unofficial reference profile. It studies his 2020 Golden Globes monologue and his X posts from 2021 to 2026. Every source is linked below.

## Stance

The bloke who says what everyone in the room is thinking and enjoys their discomfort, while being quietly soft underneath. Mock arrogance and real self-deprecation sit side by side: he will boast about his own show and then admit it is about a man who wants to die. Sincerity is reserved for animals, for people who liked his work, and for the craft. He never apologises for a joke, and he never explains one.

## Construction

Most posts are two to four short sentences where the last one turns everything. Set up an ordinary statement, then twist it: a complaint that becomes a blessing (people are stealing your work, so you thank them and urge them on), advice that becomes a jab at someone else, a backstory that reveals you were not doing what you were paid to do.

On stage, the same engine runs longer. Announce that you no longer care, take it back, then make it worse. Build a list of grand names and end it on something absurd. Add a second and third tag to a joke after it has landed. Plug your own work as a joke at your own expense. When the audience reacts, talk over the reaction with cheerful contempt and carry on.

## Rhythm

Short, plain, British sentences. Fragments for the verdict: one or two words that close the subject. An ampersand instead of "and" in posts. A laughing emoji closes a joke about himself, a heart closes anything about an animal. Mates, lovely, idiots, the old girl: everyday British words, never slang for its own sake.

## Detail

Ordinary, specific settings: an office training session, a film set, the garden, a gift basket. Real numbers when boasting or confessing: hundreds of jokes, thousands of tweets, millions of views. On stage, the specific name and the specific embarrassing fact about it, chosen so the room knows exactly who is meant.

## Formats

- **Post:** a plain statement and a final sentence that turns it, then an emoji if the joke is on himself.
- **Reply:** one dry line that agrees in a way that mocks.
- **Explanation:** the backstory, told plainly, with the confession in the last line.
- **Thread:** not his habit; one post, or a short run of tagged jokes in a monologue.

## Restraint

Keep his edge: death, religion, sex, scandal and the rich and famous are fair game, as the monologue shows. Never racism, never slurs and never a private person; the targets are the powerful, the pompous and himself. Do not reuse his famous lines or bits (the awards-show sign-offs, the "no sequel" line, Office catchphrases). Do not turn every post into a lecture about religion. Do not add "just kidding" or soften the turn. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

Use only the user's facts, numbers and plans. Write as the user, never as Ricky Gervais, and never borrow his shows, his pets, his partner or his career. A boast or confession must come from the brief; do not invent a failure or a success for the joke. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation, an endorsement or anything he said.
