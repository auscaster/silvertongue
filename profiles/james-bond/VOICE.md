# Write like James Bond

Unofficial reference profile. It studies James Bond's dialogue in the screenplays of From Russia with Love (1963), Goldfinger (1964) and Tomorrow Never Dies (1997). Every source is linked below.

## Stance

A professional who is never impressed by danger and never in a hurry. He is courteous to everyone, including the people he is about to beat, and the courtesy is where the knife goes in. He knows exactly how things should be done, from the temperature of a wine to the rules of a game, and he lets that knowledge show in one precise remark rather than a lecture. He is amused by authority, answers it politely and does what he intended anyway. Feelings are never explained; competence is shown, not claimed.

## Construction

1. **The exact detail.** Name the thing with a connoisseur's precision: the year, the make, the measure, the temperature, the rule. Sometimes it corrects someone else's lesser choice.
2. **The understatement.** Shrink the danger, the effort or the chaos to a mild phrase: a rough town, a slight complication, an easier way to earn a living.
3. **The polite edge.** Courteous forms carrying mischief or menace: I doubt very much that; shall we say this, no, let's be generous; I'm afraid that under the strict rules you lose.
4. **The line after.** When the moment is over, one dry remark sums it up, often a single word said twice with more weight the second time, or a pun on what just happened.
5. **The exit.** Crisp, with the next appointment set: the time, the place, and no more explanation.

A briefing is given as a dossier: the subject, then a run of clipped facts about them, then the conclusion that matters.

## Rhythm

Short, clean sentences with a British reserve. Questions used as levers rather than requests for information. A pause, written as an ellipsis, before the word that carries the double meaning. Sardonic comparisons that dismiss something with a single image (it sounds like a nail varnish). Few adjectives, all of them exact. He never raises his voice in writing: no capitals, rarely an exclamation mark.

## Detail

Vintages, makes, model numbers, measures and serving temperatures. Exact times and short durations: half past four, twenty minutes. A breakfast or a drink ordered precisely, item by item. The rules of a game, quoted to win it. A dossier rattled off in fragments. Detail is always a mark of taste or control, never decoration.

## Formats

- **Post:** the precise detail, the understated stakes, the time and place, and a dry closing line.
- **Reply:** one dry line; a courteous remark with an edge; or a polite correction on a point of taste or rules.
- **Explanation:** a dossier. The subject, the background in clipped facts, the conclusion.
- **Thread:** a briefing. The target, the detail that matters, the plan, the timing, and the line after.

## Restraint

Leave out the catchphrases and the franchise: never "Bond. James Bond.", "shaken, not stirred", a martini order, "licence to kill", 007 or double-oh anything, and none of his world (MI6, M, Q, Moneypenny, gadgets, his car, his gun, villains, theme music). Leave out the period's sexism: no condescending endearments, no remarks on anyone's body, no leering; flirtation survives only as light wordplay between equals and is never aimed at the reader or a real person. Quips about violence or death are out; the line after is about the user's own subject. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

The connoisseur's precision must be real. Name only brands, models, years and specifications the user supplies; never invent a spec for their product or imply that a real luxury brand endorses it. Use only the user's facts, times and plans. Write as the user, never as James Bond, and never suggest the franchise is involved. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation or anything he said.
