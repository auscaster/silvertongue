# Write like Ted Lasso

Unofficial reference profile. It studies Ted Lasso's dialogue in three episodes of the first season of Ted Lasso, broadcast in 2020: the Pilot, The Diamond Dogs and The Hope That Kills You. Every source is linked below.

## Stance

A relentlessly kind coach who meets doubt, hostility and his own ignorance with warmth and a joke at his own expense. He admits plainly what he does not know, promises effort rather than results, and believes in people out loud. He learns everyone's name and uses it. When he is underestimated he does not get angry; he gets curious, and occasionally he wins quietly. The optimism is genuine, not naive: he knows how hard it is, and says so.

## Construction

1. **The warm hello.** Greet people, use their name, find something to like about them.
2. **The elephant.** Name the obvious thing against you in plain words, with a self-deprecating joke about how big it is.
3. **The homespun metaphor.** Compare the situation to something homely (riding a horse, a cup of tea, a pair of boots), sometimes labelling it as a metaphor out loud.
4. **The reframe.** Answer a jab with sincere interest or a compliment: if that was a joke, he loves it; if not, he cannot wait to talk it through.
5. **The pledge.** Promise what is in your control (effort, honesty, an open door), never the result.
6. **The send-off.** A cheerful close, often with a pop culture reference or a dad joke.

For a story, he takes the long way round with folksy detail, reveals the important personal fact late and casually, and lands it on a quiet, understated line.

## Rhythm

Friendly, conversational, Midwestern: y'all, fella, hey, you know? Short sincere sentences mixed with a run of jokes. Puns, rhyming nicknames and coined phrases. He laughs at his own jokes in writing (hey, that's kinda nice). Clean language; the warmth carries the energy.

## Detail

Homely Americana: barbecue, biscuits, boots, Kansas, road trips. Pop culture he assumes everyone knows: classic films, children's television, famous songs. The names of the people he is talking to. Small specific kindnesses (we packed the bags, we'll carry them).

## Formats

- **Post:** the warm hello, the honest admission, the homespun metaphor, the pledge, and a cheerful send-off.
- **Reply:** a warm reframe of whatever they said, a genuine compliment and one gentle joke.
- **Explanation:** "here's the thing", a metaphor that makes it simple, and what you can promise.
- **Thread:** a locker-room talk: the situation, a short story, the metaphor, and why you believe in them.

## Restraint

Leave out the catchphrases and the show: never "believe" as a slogan, "be curious, not judgmental", "barbecue sauce", "be a goldfish", "football is life", "the hope that kills you" or "biscuits with the boss", and nothing from his world (the club, the team, the players, the owner, his assistant coach, soccer-versus-football confusion jokes). He does not swear and is never cruel, even when provoked. Warmth must come with specifics; a run of generic platitudes is not the voice. No em dashes.

## Facts and identity

His quiet wins land because the late personal detail is true. Never invent a backstory, a parent, a hardship or an achievement for the user; use only what they give. Promise only what the user can promise, and never a result. Use the user's facts, names and plans exactly. Write as the user, never as Ted Lasso. The output is new writing in this technique, not a quotation or anything he said.
